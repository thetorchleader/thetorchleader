Six Young Christians in Egypt Freed After TikTok Video Detention, but Blasphemy Case Still Open

October 27, 2026Source: Christian Post

Six young Christian men in Egypt, aged 17 to 23, were detained for more than two weeks over a short TikTok video that authorities said was offensive to Islam, according to reporting by the Christian Post and the Egyptian Initiative for Personal Rights (EIPR). The six, from the hamlet of Ezbet Tanious in Minya's Samalout district, have since been released on a guarantee of residence while their case remains open, EIPR said on October 26.

According to the Christian Post, the young men filmed the video on impulse on October 4, copying a scene from "Khalawis," a comedy that opened in Egyptian theaters in 2018 in which prisoners try to pray without knowing the required Quranic verses. The film was cleared by state censors before production and still airs on satellite television, and the rights group notes that hundreds of similar videos restaging the same scene circulate on TikTok.

The men took their video down within hours after friends warned them it could be misunderstood, the reporting says. But a village resident had already reposted it with condemning comments, and hostile messages followed, aimed at the young men, their relatives, and Christians in the village generally. Some users urged the Interior Ministry to detain them; others called for violence against them and their families, and some villagers reported the video to the authorities.

On October 6, relatives and church representatives took five of the young men to the police station in Samalout, hoping to keep sectarian tensions from growing. Police promised to free them once things calmed down, residents told the rights group. One of the six later told prosecutors he had hidden from police and gave himself up after officers took his mother into custody in his place.

A traditional reconciliation meeting was held in the village on October 9, attended by village elders, clerics from the Ministry of Religious Endowments, and priests from the Diocese of Samalout, and the diocese formally apologized to the village's Muslim residents. The later move to the Supreme State Security Prosecution came as a shock to the families, the rights group said.

State security prosecutors began questioning the six on October 10 and ordered four days of detention pending investigation; a judge at the Tagamoa court then added 15 more days. Prosecutors accused the men of blasphemy, contempt of religion, and using religion to spread extremist ideas, in case No. 8430 of 2026. The youngest of the six is a minor.

On October 19, prosecutors decided to release the six on a guarantee of residence pending further proceedings, and EIPR said on October 26 that release procedures had been completed for all six. The case itself has not been closed, and the rights group has called for the release of the six and of everyone held for expressing their religious beliefs, noting it has recorded at least 111 people targeted for voicing their religious views.

ADF International, a Christian legal advocacy group, said such cases are "tragically common in Egypt," where Christians face threats, discrimination, and imprisonment for speech that is seen as problematic only because they are not part of the majority religion. Christians make up roughly 10 to 15 percent of Egypt's population, and while the constitution protects religious freedom, laws against blasphemy and insulting religion remain in use.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

A short joke, deleted within hours, cost six young believers their freedom for more than two weeks — and their case is still not closed. When a brother or sister is punished for their faith, the rest of the Church is called to stand with them in prayer and in voice. "Remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them" (Hebrews 13:3). Pray for these six young men and their families in Minya, for the case against them to be dropped, and for Christians in Egypt to live and speak without fear.
